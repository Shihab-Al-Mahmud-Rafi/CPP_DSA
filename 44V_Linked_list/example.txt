Imagine a movie theater row with 1,000 people sitting side-by-side in numbered seats (1 to 1,000).
 [Seat 1]  [Seat 2]  [Seat 3]  ...  [Seat 1000]
  Video A   Video B   Video C         Video Z
Now, one new person (Video New) arrives and says, "I want to sit in Seat 1!"Because seats are locked side-by-side in a solid row, the person in Seat 1 cannot just disappear. 
To make room for that one single person:The person in Seat 1,000 must move back to Seat 1,001.
The person in Seat 999 must move to Seat 1,000.This chain reaction forces all 1,000 people to stand up, move, and sit down one seat over just so Seat 1 becomes empty.
That is what an array does. If you insert one item at index 0, every single existing object behind it must be physically moved over in memory. 
If your list holds thousands of massive videos, shifting them all causes massive computer lag.


The Treasure Hunt (Linked List)
Now imagine those same 1,000 people are scattered sitting anywhere they want across the entire city (heap memory). 
They are only connected because each person has a note in their pocket telling them where the next person is sitting.
 [Video A] ──────► [Video B] ──────► [Video C] ... ──────► [Video Z]

When one new person (Video New) arrives and wants to be at the front of the line:Nobody has to stand up.
Nobody has to move.You simply give Video New a note pointing to Video A's location.You announce that Video New is now the new start of the line.
 [Video New] ──► [Video A] ──► [Video B] ──► [Video C] ... ──► [Video Z]

You inserted one new item instantly by changing a single pointer. The other 1,000 massive videos stayed exactly where they were without moving a single inch.
